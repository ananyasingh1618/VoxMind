The French Revolution's immediate causes were financial and political. Decades of costly wars, including support for the American Revolution, had left the French crown deeply in debt, and attempts to tax the nobility and clergy met fierce resistance. When King Louis XVI convened the Estates-General in 1789 for the first time in over a century to address the crisis, the Third Estate, representing commoners, broke away to form the National Assembly, an act that is widely treated as the opening move of the revolution.

As the revolution radicalized, the Committee of Public Safety under Maximilien Robespierre launched the Reign of Terror in 1793 and 1794, executing thousands of perceived enemies of the revolution by guillotine. The Terror ended with Robespierre's own execution, and the resulting political instability of the Directory period eventually gave a young military officer, Napoleon Bonaparte, the opening to seize power in a 1799 coup.
